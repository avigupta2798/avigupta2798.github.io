Abstract

Abstract

Class-Incremental Learning (CIL) aims to learn new classes continuously without forgetting the previously learned ones. Recent works in CIL have significantly advanced across various modalities; however, at the intersection of audio-visual modality, this problem remains underexplored.

While representations from multimodal foundational models (MFMs) encapsulate rich static priors, our preliminary analysis shows that these representations struggle in incremental settings. Motivated by this, we propose a framework that generalizes frozen MFMs to audio-visual CIL. Specifically, we leverage their dense audio and visual representations and employ an attention-based contextual guidance module, in which the audio representations serve as task-stable contextual guidance for the visual representations, enabling effective class recognition in the incremental setting. To further mitigate catastrophic forgetting and preserve stability across tasks, we employ feature- and logit-level distillation, which are primarily responsible for the reduced forgetting. Extensive evaluations across various audio-visual CIL benchmarks demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods.

What we do

Four findings

  • 01

    Frozen MFMs — SAM-Audio, ImageBind and Qwen2.5-Omni-3B — generalize to audio-visual class-incremental learning, but their strong priors do not by themselves solve incremental recognition. Adaptation has to be targeted, not naive feature reuse.

  • 02

    Cross-modal attention uses audio as task-stable contextual guidance for visual representations. The design holds across all three foundation models, so it is a property of the method rather than of one encoder.

  • 03

    Feature- and logit-level distillation balances the current task's representations against the previous task's state, and accounts for most of the reduction in forgetting.

  • 04

    Across three benchmarks, the framework improves over both unimodal and multimodal CIL baselines — with every backbone held fixed between the baseline and our model.
